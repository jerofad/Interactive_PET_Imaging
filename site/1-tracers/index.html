Radiotracers and decay

A PET scan starts with a radiotracer: a molecule that follows a biological process, labelled with a positron-emitting isotope. The isotope decides how fast the signal fades. The molecule decides what the image means.

Chapter 1 / Tracers and decay

The isotope sets the clock.
The molecule sets the target.

In β⁺ decay, a proton becomes a neutron while a positron and an electron neutrino are emitted. The positron loses kinetic energy, then annihilates with an electron. In the dominant two-photon channel, each photon carries approximately 511 keV: the electron rest energy mₑc².

FDG follows glucose transport and phosphorylation; its retention is used to assess glucose metabolism. Amyloid ligands such as florbetapir bind to fibrillar amyloid-β. Sharing F-18 does not make these tracers measure the same biology. Deoxyglucose principle · florbetapir binding mechanism (label §12.1).

Physical decay reduces the number of radioactive nuclei. A decay correction refers measured activity back to a chosen time; it cannot replace counts that were never collected or undo biological clearance.

A(t) = A₀ · 2⁻ᵗ⁄ᵀ½
Acorrected(t) = A(t) · 2ᵗ⁄ᵀ½
Decays in window = ∫ A(t) dt

Specific activity is activity per mass, such as Bq/g. Molar activity is activity per amount, such as GBq/µmol. At the same activity, more non-radioactive compound means more injected mass and potentially more biological perturbation.

F-18, C-11 and Ga-68 half-lives are rounded teaching values from Conti & Eriksson; O-15 uses TUNL nuclear data; Rb-82 uses 75.45 s from NNDC evaluated nuclear data. The controls are simulation inputs, not dose recommendations.

Chapter 1 / Positron yield and dose

Not every decay makes a positron.

Positron fraction

Some decays proceed by electron capture and emit no positron. The positron fraction is 96.9% for F-18, 99.8% for C-11 and 99.9% for O-15. Ga-68 emits a positron in 88.9% of decays and Rb-82 in about 95%.

The readout Positrons during window above multiplies the decays by this fraction. The scanner scenes use the same fraction to scale count rates.

Positron fractions: Conti & Eriksson (2016), Tables 1 and 2. For Ga-68 and Rb-82 the table lists the two main branches.

Patient dose

Administered activity sets both the count budget and the radiation dose. For an adult FDG scan, the effective dose is roughly 0.019 mSv per MBq administered. The dose for other tracers, children and individual patients differs.

The activity sliders on this site are simulation inputs, not dose recommendations.

Dose coefficients: ICRP Publication 128 (2015).

Key takeaways

  • The half-life sets how long you can image and how quickly the count rate falls.
  • The molecule, not the isotope, determines which biology the image reflects.
  • Not every decay emits a positron. Ga-68 and Rb-82 lose noticeably more than F-18.
  • Decay correction rescales activity to a reference time. It cannot recover counts that were never detected.
