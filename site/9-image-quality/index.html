Chapter 9 of 10

Resolution, noise and quantification

Two things limit what an image can tell you. Resolution loses contrast in small objects. Noise sets how precisely you can measure uptake.

Chapter 9 / Resolution & partial volume

A small focus cannot fill its image.

Separate the physical resolution budget from the loss of contrast when an object is small compared with that resolution.

An illustrative resolution budget

Combined FWHM
DOI / parallax contribution

FWHM ≈ 1.25 √[(d/2)² + r² + (0.0022 D)² + b² + p²]

Gaussian-equivalent quadrature inspired by Moses. Here d is crystal width, r effective range blur, D ring diameter, b = 2 mm assumed decoding blur. Our parallax proxy p converts uniform depth uncertainty (20 mm, or 3 mm with DOI) to FWHM using the obliquity angle. This is a radial teaching approximation, not a scanner performance specification. Settings are independent of chapters 1–6.

Recovering a hot sphere

Centre contrast recovery
Centre activity
Solid / blurred sphereDashed / true sphere

True sphere 7 a.u.; infinite background 1 a.u. An exact 3D ball convolved with an isotropic Gaussian gives the centre profile. Recovery = (blurred centre − background) / (true sphere − background). This is centre contrast recovery, not ROI-mean recovery, SUVpeak or a fitted scanner PSF.

Model assumptions

Partial-volume effects include spill-out and spill-in. Object shape, background, motion, voxel sampling, reconstruction and ROI definition all matter. A recovery curve for one sphere model is not a general patient correction. Further reading: Soret, Bacharach & Buvat (2007).

Chapter 9 / Noise & quantification

More counts. A steadier estimate.

Count statistics determine precision. Calibration, reference time and the choice of region determine what an uptake number means.

Synthetic phantom / same axial slice

03.57 a.u. · shared scale

Choose Run to compare the same axial slice. OSEM: 3 iterations × 20 subsets; 4 mm Gaussian post-filter; all corrections enabled.

Independent Poisson acquisitions with reproducible seeds, not subsets of one event stream. The ideal relative counting standard deviation is 1/√f. Reconstructed image noise is spatially correlated and depends on the algorithm. Linear dose–time interchangeability assumes unchanged physiology and count-rate performance; dead time, randoms, decay and motion break that approximation.

What does SUV divide out?

SUVbw = C(t) / [Anet(t) / W]
With kBq/mL, MBq and kg: SUV = C × W / A

Activity concentration and net administered activity must refer to the same decay reference time. Subtract residual activity and use the appropriate calibration. Body-weight SUV is conventionally reported as dimensionless under an approximate 1 g/mL density convention; the literal mass-normalised ratio has units g/mL.

SUVmean averages a defined ROI. SUVmax selects its hottest voxel and is especially sensitive to noise, voxel size and reconstruction. SUVpeak averages a small prescribed volume and is a different measurement.

SUVR divides target-region mean uptake by reference-region mean uptake from the same acquisition. Amyloid PET often uses a tracer- and protocol-specific cerebellar reference. Reference region, acquisition window and processing must be specified; a universal positivity cutoff is inappropriate. Example: florbetapir regional uptake study.

Chapter 9 / Beyond a single number

Dynamic PET follows the tracer over time.

Static and dynamic scans

A static scan measures tissue activity in one time window, which is what SUV summarises. A dynamic scan records a series of frames from injection onwards. With the tracer concentration in arterial plasma as an input function, a compartment model can estimate rate constants for delivery, trapping and washout.

Gunn, Gunn & Cunningham (2001).

Patlak analysis

For a tracer that becomes trapped, such as FDG, a Patlak plot becomes linear after early equilibration. Its slope estimates the net influx rate Ki. SUV tracks Ki only when uptake time and plasma clearance are similar between scans.

Patlak, Blasberg & Fenstermacher (1983). This site has no dynamic model; the phantom is static.

Key takeaways

  • Objects that are not much larger than the resolution FWHM lose contrast through partial-volume effects.
  • SUV normalises activity concentration by injected activity and body weight. SUVmax is especially sensitive to noise.
  • A static SUV is a snapshot. Dynamic imaging and kinetic modelling separate delivery from uptake.
