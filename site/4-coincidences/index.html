Chapter 4 of 10

Coincidences

Two detections close enough in time are paired into a coincidence. The pair defines a line of response that the annihilation lies on, or near. Not every pair is a true coincidence.

Chapter 4 / Trues, scatter and randoms

More activity does not always
mean more useful information.

A true coincidence pairs two unscattered photons from one annihilation. A scattered coincidence includes a photon deflected in the object. A random coincidence pairs unrelated singles.

The random rate rises with both singles rates and the timing acceptance width. Narrowing the window rejects more randoms, but it must still accommodate photon flight-time differences and timing uncertainty.

W = 2τ (full timing acceptance width)
R ≈ W · S₁ · S₂ = 2τ S₁ S₂
Scatter fraction = S / (T + S)
NECR = T² / (T + S + 2R)

The 2R denominator here represents randoms estimated with an independent, equally noisy delayed window. A negligible-noise random estimate instead gives a +R term. NECR is a count-rate figure of merit, not a complete image-quality measure.

Activity here is present at scan time. It is separate from Chapter 1’s initial activity.

Some isotopes also emit a prompt gamma ray with the positron. Ga-68 emits 1077 keV in 3.2% of decays and Rb-82 emits 777 keV in 15.1%. A prompt gamma that lands in the energy window can pair with an annihilation photon and add a background that standard randoms and scatter estimates do not fully describe. This model does not include it. Source: Conti & Eriksson (2016), Table 2.

Inspect the count-rate assumptions

Let p and q be the accepted photopeak and body-tail fractions from Chapter 3. Two equivalent singles streams have Sin = 30,000 A (0.65p + 0.35q) s⁻¹, where A is in MBq. With an assumed paralyzable dead time of 40 ns, L = exp(−40 × 10⁻⁹ Sin). Rates are T = 1,500 A p²L², S = 2,000 A pqL² and R = W(SinL)², in s⁻¹. These coefficients and mixture weights are chosen teaching inputs, not fitted scanner parameters. True timing acceptance is assumed to be one throughout the available window range. No prompt-gamma or pile-up spectrum model is included.

Rate definitions and random estimation: IAEA PET/CT quality assurance. Do not use the displayed peak to choose a clinical injected activity.

Model assumptions

The 3D histories use approximate 4 mm attenuation integration and μ at 511 keV even after scattering. Random candidates pair unrelated emissions, conditioned on the timing window. The slice panels instead accumulate Poisson increments from a separate 2D model with scan-averaged rates, toy scatter shape and ideal known correction expectations. The analytic 2D-mode rate factors are assumed (0.18 for trues and an additional 0.35 for scatter); FOV uses central-point solid angle and detector depth uses exponential capture. These are illustrative scalings, not scanner specifications; this is not GATE, Geant4 or SimSET. With TOF off, an assumed 600 ps timing jitter still applies to the coincidence test for every material.

Model assumptions

t = 0 is annihilation. The decay-to-annihilation interval is shown as a conceptual prelude: positron slowing and annihilation lifetime depend on material, and positronium can introduce nanosecond delays. No universal isotope-only delay is asserted. Pulses use an assumed 0.5 ns rise, the cited material decay, a 20% CFD fraction and a 5τ energy gate. A 500 ns delayed copy illustrates the principle; it rejects this true pair. Random estimates require other, unrelated singles. These are teaching electronics, not a commercial timing chain. Key takeaways

  • Trues carry the signal. Scatter puts it on the wrong line. Randoms add a background that grows with the square of activity.
  • NECR peaks at a finite activity. Beyond the peak, more activity adds more noise than signal.
  • A delayed timing window measures the random rate directly.
