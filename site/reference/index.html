Reference / Parameter trade-offs

Every gain comes with a condition.

Direction of effect under stated assumptions. These are design relationships, not acquisition prescriptions.

Read across for the benefit, its cost and the assumption behind it.
Parameter change Potential benefit Cost or limitation
Injected activity ↑ More true coincidences in the low count-rate regime. Greater radiation exposure; randoms and dead-time losses rise. More activity need not improve NECR beyond its peak.
Energy window narrower Rejects more scattered photons. Loses accepted true events too; the balance depends on energy resolution and object scatter.
Coincidence window narrower Fewer accidental pairs; R scales with the full window width for fixed singles rates. Can reject true events if narrower than timing uncertainty and geometric delays allow.
TOF timing FWHM ↓ Better localisation along the LOR; approximate effective sensitivity gain D/Δx for a uniform object when Δx < D. Requires suitable detectors and electronics. TOF gain is task- and object-dependent, and is not a direct transverse-resolution improvement.
Crystal width ↓ Finer spatial sampling and potentially better intrinsic resolution. More channels, cost and readout complexity; light collection, stopping power and packing also matter.
DOI information ↑ Reduces uncertainty from oblique interactions, especially off-axis. Detector complexity and trade-offs in energy, timing or light collection depend on the design.
Axial field of view ↑ More solid-angle coverage and more anatomy observed simultaneously. Integrated yield can grow approximately as L² for a uniform extended source in a short-scanner regime. Not a universal sensitivity law: ideal central-point geometric acceptance is L/√(D² + L²), which saturates. Attenuation, ring differences, source extent and bed coverage change the gain; longer systems need more detector material.
Scan time ↑ More counts; ideal counting noise decreases as 1/√time for a stationary, constant-rate acquisition. Motion, radioactive decay and tracer kinetics limit the simple scaling; patient comfort and throughput matter.
Iterations / subsets ↑ More updates can recover contrast sooner. More noise and potential edge artifacts. Subsets change convergence behaviour; equal iteration × subset products do not ensure identical images.
Post-filter FWHM ↑ Smoother appearance and lower high-frequency noise. Reduced effective resolution and small-object contrast; SUVmax and partial-volume recovery change.
PSF modelling enabled May improve resolution and contrast recovery with an appropriate model. Edge overshoot, spatially varying convergence and bias from mismatch. Reconstruction settings alter quantitative comparability.

L = active axial length; D = ring diameter. The central-point expression is an ideal open cylinder’s geometric solid-angle fraction, before attenuation and detector efficiency. The L² statement combines roughly linear local acceptance with linearly increasing illuminated source extent; it is a limited-regime derivation, not a fitted scanner specification.

Reference / Glossary

Terms used on this site.

Underlined terms in the chapters link here and show these definitions on hover or keyboard focus.

Annihilation
A positron and an electron convert into, most often, two photons of about 511 keV each.
Attenuation
Loss of photons from a line of response by absorption or scattering in the body.
CTR
Coincidence timing resolution: the FWHM of the measured arrival-time difference for true pairs.
Dead time
Time during which a detector or its electronics cannot record a new event.
DOI
Depth of interaction: where along the crystal depth the photon interacted. Knowing it reduces parallax blur.
Energy window
The accepted range of measured photon energies, for example 435–650 keV.
FBP
Filtered backprojection: an analytic reconstruction that ramp-filters projections and smears them back.
FWHM
Full width at half maximum: the width of a peak at half its height.
Half-life
The time for half of the radioactive nuclei in a sample to decay.
List mode
Storage of each coincidence as a record with its detector pair, time and often energy.
LOR
Line of response: the line between the two detectors that recorded a coincidence.
Michelogram
A map of detector-ring pairs showing which axial combinations are accepted and how they are grouped.
MLEM
Maximum-likelihood expectation maximisation: an iterative reconstruction for Poisson data using all projections per update.
NECR
Noise-equivalent count rate, T²/(T+S+2R): a count-rate figure of merit that penalises scatter and randoms.
Non-collinearity
The small deviation of the two annihilation photons from exactly 180°, about 0.5° FWHM.
Normalisation
Correction for unequal efficiency between detector pairs.
OSEM
Ordered-subset EM: MLEM that updates the image with interleaved subsets of projection angles.
Partial-volume effect
Loss of apparent contrast in objects that are small relative to the resolution, through spill-out and spill-in.
Patlak analysis
A graphical method that estimates the net influx rate Ki of an irreversibly trapped tracer.
Positron range
The distance a positron travels before it annihilates.
Prompt gamma
A gamma ray emitted together with the positron by some isotopes, such as Ga-68 and Rb-82.
PSF
Point-spread function: the image of a point source. PSF modelling includes it in the system matrix.
Radiotracer
A biologically active molecule labelled with a radioactive isotope.
Randoms
Accidental coincidences between photons from two unrelated annihilations.
Scatter
Coincidences in which at least one photon changed direction in the body before detection.
Scintillator
A crystal that converts deposited gamma-ray energy into a pulse of visible light.
Sinogram
A histogram of coincidences indexed by line position s and angle φ.
SiPM
Silicon photomultiplier: a solid-state sensor that converts scintillation light into an electrical pulse.
SUV
Standardised uptake value: tissue activity concentration divided by injected activity per body weight.
SUVR
SUV ratio: mean uptake in a target region divided by mean uptake in a reference region.
System matrix
The model A whose element Aij is the expected contribution of voxel j to measurement bin i.
TOF
Time of flight: using the photons’ arrival-time difference to localise an event along its line of response.
Trues
Coincidences from one annihilation whose two photons both reached the detectors unscattered.
μ-map
An image of linear attenuation coefficients at 511 keV, used for attenuation correction.

Scope of this notebook

The embedded volume is an original analytic phantom. Ellipsoids, periodic folds and assigned tissue labels define its anatomy. GM : WM = 4 : 1, zero CSF activity and lesion activities are teaching inputs, not universal physiological ratios. Activity is in arbitrary units, not SUV.

The reconstruction lab uses one block-averaged axial slice and an ideal 2D parallel-beam geometry. Exact simulated attenuation, efficiency, scatter and randoms are available to the reconstruction. The main reconstruction lab has no scanner-specific calibration, Monte Carlo transport, TOF, PSF, motion, decay or 3D reconstruction. Separate experiments cover TOF, decay, motion and PSF mismatch. The rotating MIP projects assigned 3D activity; it does not add a 3D reconstruction model.

The count sweep reconstructs noisy projections. Its relative counting-noise readout, √(10⁶/N), describes ideal count scaling relative to 10⁶ prompts; it is not a measured image-noise or NECR metric.

Numerical and worker checks cover the projection engine and teaching models. Further checks cover sphere recovery, unit conversion, positron fractions, detector capture and count-rate scaling. They run as part of the automated test suite.

Chapters 1–6 add independent teaching models for decay, range, detection, count rates, attenuation and TOF. Only the explicit “Try attenuation on and off in the lab” link changes reconstruction-lab settings. Later chapters add reconstruction statistics, partial-volume recovery, hypothetical SUV/SUVR, eight artifact modes and a rotating MIP. The four 3D scenes add recording formats, fullscreen and text fallbacks.
