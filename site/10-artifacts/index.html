Model assumptions

The source is a brain phantom. Periodic motion and scatter overcorrection are analogues of mechanisms seen in respiratory imaging and near high-activity organs such as the bladder; no chest or bladder is present here. Artificial μ-map bands and voids illustrate attenuation errors without simulating CT beam hardening or MR signal formation. Chapter 10 / Motion in practice

Patients breathe during a scan.

Respiratory gating

Breathing moves lesions in the chest and upper abdomen during a scan that lasts minutes. Gating uses list-mode timing to sort events by breathing phase. Each gate is sharper but holds fewer counts, so it is noisier. Motion-compensated reconstruction can combine the gates again.

Mismatch with CT

A CT is acquired in seconds, often at one breathing phase, while PET averages over many breaths. The attenuation map can therefore disagree with the PET near the diaphragm. The misregistration experiment above shows the mechanism in the brain phantom.

Nehmeh & Erdi (2008).

Key takeaways

  • Motion blurs activity and can misalign the attenuation map.
  • Errors in the μ-map bias activity along every line that crosses the error, not only where the map is wrong. Metal, truncation and MR voids are examples.
  • PSF modelling can sharpen edges, but overcorrection creates overshoot.
