Chapter 6 of 10

Time of flight

If the scanner measures which photon arrived first, and by how much, it can place each event on a segment of its line rather than anywhere along it. This is time of flight.

Chapter 6 / Time of flight

A timing difference narrows
where an event could have happened.

A timing offset places the centre of the event’s localisation kernel along the LOR. Timing resolution sets the width of that kernel. These are separate quantities.

x = c · (tleft − tright) / 2
Localisation FWHM Δx = c · CTR / 2
c = 0.299792458 mm/ps

With this sign convention, a positive delay places the event to the right. A 200 ps coincidence timing resolution gives a localisation FWHM of about 30 mm. It does not make the reconstructed image resolution 30 mm; many LORs constrain the image together.

For a roughly uniform object, a first estimate of effective sensitivity gain is D/Δx, with an SNR gain of its square root. D is the object diameter, not the scanner-ring diameter. This estimate is useful when Δx is smaller than the object.

Effective gain ≈ max(1, D/Δx)
SNR gain ≈ √(effective gain)

TOF localisation and approximate gain: Surti (2015). Exact reconstruction gains depend on the object, count level, timing model and algorithm; they are not guaranteed by this ratio.

Key takeaways

  • Localisation FWHM is c × CTR ÷ 2. A 200 ps CTR gives about 30 mm.
  • TOF mainly reduces noise by raising effective sensitivity. It is not a direct gain in spatial resolution.
  • The benefit grows with object size, so larger patients gain more.
