Reconstruction

Reconstruction turns counts into an image. It searches for the activity that, passed through a model of the scanner (the system matrix), best explains the measured sinogram.

Chapter 8 / Reconstruction in depth

A model, an estimate, a correction.

Every update asks whether a proposed activity image could have produced the measured coincidences.

The system matrix is the bridge

For projection bin i and voxel j, Aij is the expected contribution per unit activity. Here Siddon’s ray intersections give the path length in each square voxel. Exposure, detector efficiency and the attenuation survival factor multiply each row. Scatter and randoms enter as additive expected counts b.

yᵢ ~ Poisson((Ax)ᵢ + bᵢ)
xⱼ ← xⱼ / (Aᵀ1)ⱼ · [Aᵀ(y / (Ax + b))]ⱼ

The transpose Aᵀ uses exactly the same path lengths as the forward projector. Sensitivity Aᵀ1 accounts for unequal sampling. In this notebook the matrix is stored sparsely; a ray only touches a small fraction of the image.

FBP, MLEM and OSEM

FBP corrects the projections, applies a ramp filter and backprojects. It is fast and linear; its ramp amplifies high spatial frequencies and noise, and negative estimates are possible.

MLEM uses all projections per update, preserves non-negativity and increases the Poisson likelihood for this matched model. More iterations recover detail but can amplify noise.

OSEM updates with interleaved angular subsets and their own sensitivity images. One pass through all subsets is one iteration. Faster initial progress does not guarantee full-data likelihood increases at every update; fixed subsets can approach a limit cycle.

Early stopping and regularisation

Early stopping and smoothing trade detail for lower noise. The graph estimates bias from the ensemble mean and spread about that mean; RMS error² = bias² + spread², all normalised by the truth’s squared norm. Eight samples give a noisy finite-ensemble estimate, not a universal optimum or an uncertainty interval.

x̂ = arg maxx ≥ 0 { log p(y | x) − β R(x) }

A penalty R can favour smoothness or preserve edges. Increasing β usually suppresses noise and can suppress true small structures too. Penalised likelihood is explained here; the lab itself implements unpenalised EM with an optional post-filter.

Resolution models and learned updates

A point-spread model can be placed inside A. Correct modelling may improve recovery; sharp boundaries, imperfect kernels and excessive iteration can produce edge overshoot. Inspect the PSF mismatch experiment in chapter 10.

FBSEM couples forward–backward splitting and EM with learned regularisation. TransEM embeds a residual Swin-transformer regulariser in iterative PET reconstruction. These research methods combine a measurement model with learned image information; results depend on training data, count level and evaluation task. Neither network is run by this notebook.

Model assumptions

Plain backprojection spreads each line over the image, producing the familiar 1/r blur in the ideal continuous 2D case. Its frames are peak-normalised for display. FBP and iterative frames retain activity scaling. Act 2’s backprojected ratio uses all angles to diagnose the next full-data correction, even when OSEM updates use subsets. Decay correction refers the estimate to the phantom’s assigned reference activity; it does not restore lost counts.

Key takeaways

  • FBP is fast and linear but amplifies noise. MLEM and OSEM model Poisson counts and stay non-negative.
  • More iterations lower bias and raise noise. The stopping point and the post-filter are choices, not defaults to trust.
  • Every correction lives in the model. A wrong model produces a confident, wrong image.
