Positron range and non-collinearity

The tracer emits a positron, not a photon. The positron travels a short distance and annihilates with an electron. Two 511 keV photons then leave in nearly opposite directions. Both steps blur the image before any detector is involved.

Chapter 2 / Positron range and non-collinearity

The annihilation is not exactly
where the decay happened.

The positron travels before annihilation. Its displacement depends on its energy and the material it travels through. Mean travel range and the FWHM of the resulting image blur are different quantities.

The two photon directions are also slightly non-collinear. A larger detector ring turns the same angular deviation into a larger position error.

Non-collinearity blur FWHM ≈ 0.0022 D
At the centre: blur ≈ D · δ / 4
δ ≈ 0.0088 rad ≈ 0.50° FWHM

Here δ is the FWHM of the photon-pair angle’s deviation from 180°. Some texts write the same spread as ±0.25°; that is half of the 0.5° full width, not a different value.

Effective range-blur widths and ring dependence: Moses (2011). Mean water ranges: Conti & Eriksson (2016). Ga-68 and Rb-82 means refer to their dominant positron branches.

Key takeaways

  • Positron range blurs the image, much more for Ga-68 and Rb-82 than for F-18.
  • Non-collinearity adds blur that grows with ring diameter, about 0.0022 × D.
  • Both are physical limits that better detectors cannot remove. A smaller ring reduces non-collinearity blur, and reconstruction can partly model positron range.
